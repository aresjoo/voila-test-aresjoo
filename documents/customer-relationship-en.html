How are results decided, and how does gotit.gg make money?

gotit.gg decides every result with a number that you can recompute yourself, and each Box is built to return 99% of its price in Item Value. At the same time, the way we make money is written into the same published numbers. You should understand and ask us about these numbers because they decide what you win and what we keep. Here is how each part works:

The Odds Table. Each Box has its own table of 1,000,000 slots. Every Item in the Box holds a whole number of those slots, so the odds of an Item are its number of slots out of 1,000,000, and those odds are shown on the page of the Box.

The Result Number. Before you open a Box, we show you the SHA-256 hash of our server seed. The result is calculated as HMAC-SHA256(serverSeed, clientSeed:nonce). The first 8 hex characters of that output are converted to an integer, and the remainder after dividing by 1,000,000 is your result number. That number points to one slot in the table of the Box, and the Item that holds the slot is the Item you win.

Return on Boxes. gotit.gg earns revenue from the Boxes you open. Specifically, each Box is built so that the Item Value it returns is 99% of its price on average, and gotit.gg keeps the remaining 1% of the Box price.

Upgrades. Your chance of success in an upgrade is 0.9 multiplied by the Item Value you put up, divided by the Item Value of the target, and the target can be worth 1.2 times to 40 times the Item you put up. gotit.gg keeps 10% on upgrades, which is the difference between 1 and 0.9.

Everything gotit.gg Receives: - 1% of the price of each Box you open. - 10% on upgrades. - Withdrawal fees of 1.00 USDT on TRC-20 and 0.50 USDT on BEP-20.

Taking Item Value in USDT. gotit.gg does not receive anything when you take an Item in USDT. You receive 100% of the Item Value, whether the Item came from a $20 Box or is the $5,800,000 Bugatti Divo from the Supercar Box.

How can I check a result myself?

Generally, every result on gotit.gg can be recomputed by you from three values: the server seed, your client seed, and the nonce. Before you open a Box, we show you the SHA-256 hash of the server seed, so the value that decides your result is fixed before you open. To check a result, compute HMAC-SHA256 with the server seed as the key and the client seed and the nonce, joined by a colon, as the message. Take the first 8 hex characters of the output, convert them to an integer, and divide by 1,000,000. The remainder is your result number. Additionally, you can compare that number with the table of the Box you opened, where each Item holds a whole number of the 1,000,000 slots, and compute the SHA-256 hash of the server seed to confirm that it matches the hash you were shown before you opened. The same calculation applies to all 320 Boxes, from a $20 Box to the Supercar Box.

6.3 The calculation. We calculate the draw with HMAC-SHA256. In plain text, the formula is: roll = the first 8 hex characters of HMAC-SHA256(serverSeed, clientSeed:nonce), read as an integer, mod 1000000. The algorithm identifier is hmac-sha256-mod1e6-v1. The roll is the result number used in the next part of the process.

(a) Use the server seed as the key for HMAC-SHA256. Form the message by joining the recorded client seed, a colon, and the recorded nonce. Apply HMAC-SHA256 to that key and message. The result of this operation is the hexadecimal output from which the result number is derived.

(b) Read the first 8 hexadecimal characters of that output. Convert those characters into an integer. This means interpreting their hexadecimal value as a number. It does not mean adding the characters together or taking the first characters of a decimal rendering. The formula specifies which part of the HMAC output to use and how to read it.

(c) Divide the integer by 1,000,000 and keep the remainder. The word “mod” in the formula means this remainder operation. The result lies from 0 through 999,999. These possible numbers correspond to the 1,000,000 positions used by the Box table.

As a remainder example, an integer of 1,234,567 leaves 234,567 after division by 1,000,000. This example explains the last arithmetic step.
